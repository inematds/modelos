The models, in plain language

What each model is for

Many models arrived at once and it's easy to feel overwhelmed. Here, without jargon, is what each one does well, when to use it and when to avoid it.

Opus 5.5 Back to the guide

🟠 Claude Opus 5.5 In the stack · General use — the main one

The best for everyday work: fast, direct, token-efficient and excellent on complex projects.

Think of it as the senior professional who handles almost anything with little explanation.

Use it for
  • General work and large projects
  • Websites, video, design and creative tasks
  • Research and browser use
  • Second opinion on ambiguous cases
Avoid it for
  • Repetitive code fixes with a clear criterion — Sol does it for less
Effort: low effort (needs few instructions)

🔷 GPT-6 Astra In the stack · Planning and hard problems

The model for thinking before doing: planning, deciding architecture and tackling what is truly hard.

Think of it as the architect: it draws the plan someone else will execute.

Use it for
  • Planning projects and simulating scenarios
  • Hard and architectural decisions
  • First proposal on an ambiguous case (with Opus reviewing)
Avoid it for
  • Repetitive grunt work — a waste
Effort: medium to high effort (no gain above that)

☀️ GPT-6 Sol In the stack · Cost-effective execution

Good and token-efficient, but below Astra on demanding work. It shines when the task is already well defined.

Think of it as the efficient executor: it follows the plan to the letter.

Use it for
  • Executing a ready plan (plan with Astra → execute with Sol)
  • Reviewing, running and fixing code with tests
  • Worker under an Opus 5.5 orchestrator
Avoid it for
  • Creative tasks, design, video and browser
  • Hard problems without a plan
Effort: medium to high effort

🌙 GPT-6 Luna In the stack · Grunt work

The executor for repetitive, clear and unambiguous tasks. It has two gears: light and heavy.

Think of it as the assistant who handles the volume, as long as the instruction is exact.

Use it for
  • Luna Low: simple, fast tasks (rename, extract, format)
  • Luna High / X-High: heavy operational work, large batches
Avoid it for
  • Anything that requires interpreting what you meant
Effort: Low for simple; High / X-High for heavy

💎 Claude Fable 5.1 Out of the stack · Lost its place

Very capable, but token-hungry — and Opus 5.5 now delivers the same or more for much less.

Think of it as the expensive consultant you only call when nothing else works.

Use it for
  • For an ambiguous case, prefer Astra + Opus 5.5 as a second opinion
Avoid it for
  • General use — too heavy on the subscription quota
Effort: out of the stack

✖️ Grok 4.7 Out of the stack · A step back

Poor experience in hands-on use and worse than the previous version (4.6).

If you need Grok, 4.6 was better.

Avoid it for
  • Everything, for now
Effort: out of the stack

➖ Claude Sonnet 5 · Haiku 4.5 Out of the stack · Skip in the Claude stack

Sonnet 5 costs too much per task; the current Haiku is weak. Inside Claude, use Opus 5.5 at low effort.

Their role moved to others: Luna does grunt work, Opus at low effort does the general work.

Use it for
  • Haiku: only as a very cheap subagent in a 100% Claude flow — test first
Avoid it for
  • Main stack
Effort: out of the stack

⚖️ Main rule: don't trust benchmarks alone

Test the models on your own tasks.

Beware of the honeymoon effect: new models look amazing in their first days. Wait a week and watch stability, quality and cost before changing your whole stack.

🌊 If you feel overwhelmed

You're not alone. Instead of getting lost in benchmark noise, run a quick test: three of your real tasks, two models, the same request. In half an hour you'll know more than after a day of reading comparisons.
